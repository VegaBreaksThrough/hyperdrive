To dive deeper into imaginary time, it helps to understand its roots in mathematics and physics and its purpose in cosmological theories.

### The Mathematical Concept of Imaginary Time

Imaginary time stems from complex numbers in mathematics, where numbers include both real and imaginary components. An imaginary number involves the square root of -1, commonly represented as "i." In this context, imaginary time is not "imaginary" in the sense of being unreal, but rather a mathematically distinct way of describing time.

In ordinary, or real time, time moves forward in a linear fashion, giving us a clear past, present, and future. However, in complex systems—like those Hawking studied in quantum cosmology—using imaginary numbers allows equations to function differently. By converting time into an imaginary number (multiplying time by the square root of -1), we can model time as another spatial dimension.

### Why Imaginary Time?

One of the key issues in theoretical physics has been reconciling the big questions in cosmology, particularly around the Big Bang. Under general relativity, which describes gravity and large-scale cosmic structure, time behaves like a straight line, marching forward. But near the Big Bang or in black holes, these equations break down, leading to singularities—points of infinite density where the laws of physics as we know them cease to apply.

Imaginary time offers a workaround to these singularities. When we "rotate" time to the imaginary axis in complex mathematics (called a Wick rotation), time no longer behaves as a linear arrow but as a circular, closed structure without beginning or end. This smooths out the infinite "edge" of time that would otherwise occur at the Big Bang. Instead of a sharp origin point, we get a continuous, finite, "rounded" surface, much like the surface of a sphere.

### Imaginary Time and the No-Boundary Proposal

With physicist James Hartle, Hawking developed the no-boundary proposal, an idea suggesting that the universe has no beginning or end in the usual sense. Instead, using imaginary time, the universe can be described as finite but unbounded—meaning it is closed upon itself like a sphere. Just as a sphere has no edges, the universe in imaginary time has no point where time "starts" or "stops."

To picture this, imagine Earth as a 2D analogy. Walking along its surface, you never encounter an edge; it’s continuous. Imaginary time proposes something similar for the universe: a finite but boundary-less model, eliminating the paradox of "what came before the Big Bang." In this model, imaginary time stretches out, allowing a description of the universe that sidesteps the need for a singular origin. The Big Bang, rather than a single point of creation, is instead a phase transition—a change in states that doesn’t demand a "before" or "after" in the traditional sense.

The Implications of Imaginary Time

1. Unified Physical Theories: Imaginary time is vital to Hawking’s quest for a theory of everything, as it helps unify quantum mechanics (which describes the very small) with general relativity (which describes the very large).


2. Infinite Past and Future: In imaginary time, there’s no need for a fixed point where the universe begins, which challenges our intuitive conceptions of time. The universe could, in theory, be "eternal" in both directions.


3. Alternative Universes and Quantum Cosmology: Imaginary time also plays a role in quantum cosmology, suggesting multiple possible histories or "paths" for the universe. Imaginary time is a foundation for these quantum descriptions, where the universe might have countless possible "pasts" and "futures" that could influence its current state.


4. Avoiding Singularity Issues: One of the most intriguing aspects of imaginary time is its ability to remove singularities (infinite points), which defy the laws of physics. Instead of reaching an "end" at a black hole or Big Bang, imaginary time enables a smooth continuation, circumventing the mathematical "edges" where physics traditionally breaks down.



### Visualizing Imaginary Time

While challenging to visualize directly, you might imagine imaginary time as a dimension that wraps around, like the circumference of a circle, compared to real time, which would be like the line along the diameter. In this sense, imaginary time forms a continuous loop, much like seasons or cycles in nature, without a hard start or stop.

### Conclusion

Imaginary time is a profound shift in our understanding of time, treating it as more fluid and dynamic, allowing theoretical physics to describe the cosmos without traditional boundaries. It enables us to imagine a universe that is whole and self-contained, bypassing the need for an external origin and giving a glimpse of how time and space might be infinitely interwoven at the deepest levels of reality.
