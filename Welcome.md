### The Graph
Point in case, any article you read will tell you what it is linked to with the graph on the upper right. You can expand that graph full screen with the arrow or access the entire HyperDrive's Global Graph with the button next to the arrow.

### The Folders
To your left, the master folders. 
There are not going to be more folders than this, since there are already grouping of energies within arrangings, collections and artifacts. Thus, it seemed to me that this was the most natural and simple layout for you to find your way.
The effort here, ultimately, is to implement what the IT world calls a "flat structure". There is a tendency to view the world hierarchically, with sets within sets, etc... This "librarian" view based on our three dimensional perception of the world (and what was possible with paper) is a limitation. 
The flat structure is a way of linking information where all sets crossover each other, which the typical folder structure does not allow for. That is why the next phase of development will be the use of #tags instead, grouping ideas across the HyperDrive based on topic, theme, keyword, ... In this way, one note can belong in a series of sets without the need for the folder structure.

***But there is a better way.***

### The Search Bar
Your best ally. In todays IT world, the search bar of every Operating System, software or website will get you where you need to go the **fastest**. You can type any arranging, energy, artefact etc... that you want to find out about in the search bar on top of the folders. Beware that this database was written entirely in English, which means you will have to type in the original name in English, even if you are using the translation feature in your navigator.
