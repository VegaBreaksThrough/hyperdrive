This comment, ‘Wrong Thinking Harry’, addresses the entire falsity of the usual paradigm and the tragic consequences that flow from this point of view. Paramount among these is that our consciousness is merely an epiphenomenon… whereas actually everything is arising within consciousness, not visa versa.
‘Wrong Thinking Harry’ considers the fundamental source of all this error, some of which is the structure of the brain, i.e. our sensory apprehension, which gives the appearance of everything being targeted in the head, and the head, therefore, becoming the epitome of the body in the common view, and in this view, the heart is sacrificed to the head, which is to say our real life is sacrificed to a false life, and we are worshipping a false god in this way. The true case here is that the head is secondary to the heart and therefore we must [[sacrifice]] the head to the heart, since the heart is the source of consciousness. Our thoughts that loom large within the cultures of the Western mind are merely subtle objects that in Truth are not even particularly interesting and are certainly not worthy of our obsessive fascination. The foundation of our thinking is not some great illuminating notion, but rather the isolation of the bereft lost in their separative activity beyond the heart of the world. These thoughts, these subtle objects, are simply arising, i.e. we do not think them, they are simply occurring, mostly out of the mire and muck of our souls. Consequently, our thoughts are unworthy objects to savour in lengthy regard, in the museum of our troubled existence.
Real living is more like satori, than mulling around in the [[muttering dark]]. It behoves us to cease our fascination with all this falsity and stand free within the heart’s recognition of the Truth of us, which is revealed when we embrace the heart of [[Truth]], rather than an obsequious relinquishment of creative control to the thought daemons of our recalcitrant head, the [[The Dalek Invasion|Daleks]] of the soul, the ‘Wrong Thinking Harry.’

The phrase **“Wrong Thinking, Harry”** originates from the **1945 film _The Picture of Dorian Gray_**, based on the novel by Oscar Wilde. It is spoken by the character **Lord Henry Wotton**, often referred to as “Harry.”
![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dv95Rt8cHCk)

  

**Context:**

  

In both the novel and the film, **Lord Henry Wotton** is a hedonistic and cynical aristocrat who influences Dorian Gray with his philosophy of indulgence and aestheticism. The phrase **“Wrong Thinking, Harry”** is part of the dialogue where **Basil Hallward**, the painter and moral conscience of the story, criticizes Lord Henry for his manipulative and amoral influence on Dorian.

  

The line encapsulates the tension between the characters:

• **Lord Henry** represents the seductive allure of hedonism and moral relativism.

• **Basil Hallward** represents the more traditional, moral viewpoint.

  

**Cultural Resonance:**

  

The phrase has since been used metaphorically to critique or humorously call out flawed reasoning or amoral perspectives, particularly when echoing the intellectual and philosophical themes of Wilde’s work.
