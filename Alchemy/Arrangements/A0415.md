Horus: Irradie et nettoie.

Horus is one of the most important and complex deities in [[Gods of Ancient Egypt|ancient Egyptian mythology]], associated with kingship, the sky, and protection. He is often depicted as a falcon or a man with a falcon's head, symbolizing his dominion over the sky. Horus was worshipped from the earliest periods of Egyptian history and played a central role in both myth and the religious practices of the time.

### **Fundamentals of Horus**

#### 1. **Origins and Mythological Background**
- **Parentage:** Horus is the son of Osiris, the god of the afterlife, and Isis, the goddess of magic and motherhood. His birth is significant in Egyptian mythology, especially in the myth of Osiris, where he is born after Isis magically revives Osiris.

- **Role in the Osiris Myth:** The most famous myth involving Horus is his battle with his uncle Set, who murdered Osiris. Horus avenges his father's death, defeats Set, and reclaims the throne of Egypt, symbolizing the triumph of order over chaos.

#### 2. **Forms of Horus**
- **Horus the Elder (Horus the Great):** This aspect of Horus is seen as the sky god, a direct personification of the sky itself, with his eyes representing the [[A0437|sun]] and the [[A0457|moon]]. He is one of the oldest deities in the [[Gods of Ancient Egypt|Egyptian pantheon]].
  
- **Horus the Younger (Horus son of Isis):** This is the form of Horus most commonly associated with the Osiris myth. He is depicted as the avenger of his father and the rightful king of Egypt.

- **Horus the Child (Harpocrates):** Depicted as a young boy with a finger to his mouth, this form of Horus represents the newborn [[A0437|sun]] and is associated with childhood and vulnerability.

#### 3. **Symbols and Iconography**
- **Falcon:** Horus is often depicted as a falcon or a man with a falcon's head, symbolizing his dominion over the sky and his role as a protector of the realm.

- **Eye of Horus:** Also known as the "Wedjat," this symbol represents protection, royal power, and good health. It is derived from the myth where Horus's eye was injured in his battle with Set and later restored.

- **Double Crown:** Horus is frequently shown wearing the double crown of Upper and Lower Egypt, symbolizing his role as the unifier and ruler of all Egypt.

#### 4. **Roles and Functions**
- **God of Kingship:** Horus is the divine prototype of the pharaoh, who was considered the living incarnation of Horus on earth. This association underscores the divine legitimacy of the pharaoh’s rule.

- **God of the Sky:** As a sky god, Horus's eyes were believed to be the [[A0437|sun]] and the [[A0457|moon]], making him a [[The Hermetic Gods|celestial]] deity responsible for the natural order and the passage of time.

- **Protector and Avenger:** Horus is seen as a protector of the people and the ruler of Egypt. His battles against Set symbolize the ongoing struggle between order (maat) and chaos (isfet), with Horus representing the force that maintains order.

#### 5. **Worship and Temples**
- **Cult Centers:** Horus was worshipped across Egypt, but his primary cult center was in Edfu, where the Temple of Horus stands as one of the best-preserved ancient temples. This temple was dedicated to Horus as the god of kingship and sky.

- **Festivals:** Various festivals celebrated Horus’s victories over Set, reinforcing his role as the protector of Egypt and the pharaoh. These festivals often included reenactments of his mythological battles.

#### 6. **Legacy and Influence**
- **Horus and the Pharaoh:** The pharaoh was seen as the earthly embodiment of Horus, and his rule was legitimized through this divine connection. This identification reinforced the pharaoh’s role as the protector and unifier of Egypt.

- **Symbol of Protection:** The Eye of Horus became a powerful amulet used by Egyptians for protection and healing, extending Horus’s influence into daily life.

- **Syncretism:** Over time, Horus was merged with other deities, such as Ra, creating composite gods like Ra-Horakhty, who embodied both the [[A0437|solar]] and royal aspects of Egyptian religion.

#### 7. **Comparison with Other Deities**
- **Ra:** While Ra is the primary [[A0437|sun]] god, Horus also has [[A0437|solar]] aspects, particularly in his form as Ra-Horakhty, which combines the attributes of both gods.

- **Set:** As Horus’s main antagonist, Set represents chaos and disorder. Their conflict is one of the central narratives of Egyptian mythology, symbolizing the struggle between good and evil.

Horus is a central figure in Egyptian mythology, embodying kingship, protection, and the natural order. His myths and symbols have had a lasting impact on Egyptian religion, and his role as the divine protector of the pharaoh made him one of the most venerated deities in ancient Egypt.
