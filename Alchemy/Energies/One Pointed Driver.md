Many of us find that the vicissitudes of driving in traffic are distracting and confusing. In this array of vehicles speeding, chopping and changing lanes we often find ourselves suffering as victims of the old adage, "he who hesitates is lost," and the lostness referred to here could be calamitous. The usual driver is a coper, a 'Dalek' of the highway, sometimes literally an annihilator, obliterator and exterminator as we see with the ever growing numbers of 'punk drivers' and their mag wheels and 'road ragers' with blood clots for brains.
One Pointed Driver may never reach these 'would be' road warriors attempting to emulate 'Mad Max', but it can help those who find their way to this support by inviting conscious one-pointedness, wherein attention is maintained as a matter of discipline at the root of the nose or at the seat of consciousness relative to the body.
When we have gained greater creative control over this situation the previously confusing and high speed traffic can appear to move into slow motion and now have more time, it seems, to respond to the traffic display that surrounds us. Being able to control a vehicle depends on our energy being greater than the 'nervous energy' requirements of the vehicle. When our energy falls below a certain point, we present ourselves as a menace on the highway, since in weakened or intoxicated states we are overwhelmed by the demand of the driving situation. One Pointed Driver, as well as focusing our attention, is a reminder to lift our energy to the energy requirements of being a safe creative driver.
