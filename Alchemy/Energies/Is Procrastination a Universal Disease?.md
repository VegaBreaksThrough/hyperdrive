“Mañana is good enough for me” is the ‘catch cry’ of millions as they bump along in a conga line that already reaches into tomorrow. Without siding with the ‘wet blanket wowsers’, what is at odds with real life is that ‘mañana’ is taken on as a viable philosophy of life; a counter resurgence against the workaholics as its major impetus - “Hey mun what’s ya hurry?” - which would have us believe that the holy grail of life is perhaps the seductive wafting of a certain aromatic weed and the horn of plenty brimming with piña colada. This then is one form of procrastination, which is for those with low anxiety. An old joke from the beatnik era perhaps sums up the mañana folks and their position, “I’m not really lazy, I’m just overcompensating for the tendency to become a compulsive worker!” Whatever is the case, the supposed merriment of the conga line actually attempts to hide a vast disturbance. The supposed ‘life-positive’ pose is not based upon any penetration of the ‘actual’ circumstances of life or what the real involvement with this place really needs to be. The ‘mañana’ philosophy is a hedonistic distraction away from any serious consideration of the mortal position which we are all faced with. “Mañana” seeks to avoid any demand for real functioning, because the usual person cannot tolerate any demand on their time or energy when this demand becomes forever, or sometimes, even for a moment. 
The anxious cases are swift to tell us, “Mañana, I wish!” and that they are, “really on top of everything” and “taking care of business!” The anxieties of the upwardly mobile force them into an orgy of distraction, usually involving money matters and what is their status in the flow of the money pipeline. Is ‘plenty’ coming their way, are they ‘where’ it matters? The ‘money chasers’ are a disturbance hiding a vast disturbance, or attempting to. Again, the lifepositive pose is not based on any real consideration of our circumstance. In this case it is part of the job description and rows of pictures line the corridors of fame of those who have ‘made it’ and whose very serious countenances suggest very positively “If we can make it, so can you.”
Of course, there are very many people who are not capable of being ‘bright, young and positive!’ and who drift around in ‘mediocre land’ buying lottery tickets. Even the heavy karmas of the poor of the third world have somehow opted for their own form of distraction, which is the demands for mere survival. It is true, that our karmas can be such that we have ‘no hope’ in this lifetime of anything greater than worldly circumstances. Those of us who have no serious impediments and who could generate the time for ‘serious’ consideration of what real life amounts to and who fail to generate such time, are procrastinating. If the first world culture could see its greatest value lies in the ‘free time’ it has created, beyond the needs of mere survival, then the usual case of, ‘taking care of business’, where the impulse toward ‘free time’ is overpowered by the means, that is, we have forgotten why we needed ‘free time’, would not exist. Failing to; getting around to; doing anything about this dramatisation of forgetfulness has a hidden belief that there will always be a mañana in which we may finally “Take heed...”. In our procrastination we are falsely imbued with the belief of our own immortality and in the fantasy of endless time we have forgotten we need ‘free’ time for the real consideration of real life and not for surfing the money pipeline. Hence, because we fail to turn our time and energy beyond the trivia of existence into the demands of real life, the transcendence of conventional life, we are all suffering the disease of procrastination, where at any time all our plans crumble to ruin, when the body lets us down, by suddenly dropping dead and denying us the trivia of another mañana.
