**Amentis** is a term with roots in ancient Egyptian esoteric and mystical traditions, particularly as interpreted by figures like R.A. Schwaller de Lubicz. It is associated with the concept of the **underworld or the hidden realms**, often linked to the Egyptian **Duat**, which represents a metaphysical space of transformation and regeneration.

  

In this context, Amentis can symbolize:

1. **The Hidden or Unseen Realm**: A domain beyond ordinary perception where spiritual and metaphysical processes unfold.

2. **The Inner World**: Representing the subconscious or inner dimensions of the psyche, where the mysteries of the self are accessed.

3. **A Space of Renewal**: Often tied to the cycles of death and rebirth, reflecting the idea of transformation and the journey toward higher consciousness.

  

The term **Amentis** originates from ancient Egyptian cosmology and language, where it is connected to the word **Amentet** or **Amenti**, often translated as “the hidden land,” “the west,” or “the land of the dead.”

  

**Cultural and Linguistic Roots**

1. **Amentet (Egyptian Mythology):**

• Amentet is associated with the west, where the sun sets, symbolizing death, transition, and the entrance to the afterlife.

• It was also personified as a goddess, often depicted as welcoming souls into the afterlife or underworld.

2. **The Duat:**

• Amentet overlaps conceptually with the **Duat**, the Egyptian underworld, where the soul undergoes judgment and transformation. This realm was both a literal and metaphysical space, essential in the journey toward immortality and unity with the divine.

  

**Esoteric Interpretations**

  

In mystical traditions like those of R.A. Schwaller de Lubicz, Amentis represents not only the realm of the dead but also a metaphysical state or dimension. It embodies:

• **The Hidden Wisdom of the Inner World**: A space of spiritual introspection and transformation.

• **A Universal Principle**: Reflecting the cyclical nature of life, death, and rebirth.
