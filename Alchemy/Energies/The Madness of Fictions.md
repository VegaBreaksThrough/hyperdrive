There have been many books that have considered the “as ifs” of our ‘thinking’, i.e. all the ‘things’ we act upon, “as if” they were true, such as the ‘fictions’ of mathematics, science, statistics and logic. What we see is the foundation of all our ‘formal thinking’ is merely ‘fictional’. When we stray from ‘formal thinking’ into the morass of what people ‘hold to be true’, that simply isn’t, we find ourselves entering ‘The Twilight Zone’ of the unconsidered so-called ‘truths’ of what we merely like to believe and therefore what ‘is true’ is only ‘emotional’, a ‘preference’ wherein all the ‘evils’ of the world reside.

What the writings have considered is many of the prominent and mad fictions of the conventional world of our dreaming, i.e. the entire structure of our ordinary lives is simply ‘dreamed up’. Is it any wonder that many of us have ‘dreamed up’ some really mad stuff? There is also a vast ‘reluctance’ to give up this ‘dreamed up stuff’. Probably the maddest something we have ‘dreamed up’ is the idea of self, for which there is absolutely no evidence and it is this mad belief that is responsible for all the ‘ills’ of the world. The belief that we are ‘selved’ is a naive presumption based upon the mere appearance of all that is arising, which goes like this, “I am in a body”, in fact, “I am in the head of the body”, “The body is in the world”, “The world is in the [[Fire from the Heart of the Solar System|solar system]]” and so on. Making the head the epitome of the body is erroneously based upon listening to the [[muttering dark]] that appears to be focused in the head and it is because of this that we feel we are a ‘me’, a self, ‘a someone’. [[P0749|Ramana Maharshi]] points out that the head cannot be the epitome of the body, because when we fall asleep, the head is the first to drop. Therefore the self is a complete and utter madness and if we look upon the world at large we see that this is true. From the mad notion of self we have developed all the weirdness of self-improvement, self-esteem, self-development, self-acceptance, self-denial, self, self, self... We cling to this notion desperately, because we ‘think’ that beyond our self-definitions, madness lies. The self is already mad, so what kind of madness are we talking of? Is it the raving, screaming, straight-jacket kind, with men in white jackets sticking needles into us? Isn’t this the vision of madness of what actually lies below our self-definitions? So is this mad notion of self in part an attempt to remain ‘cool’ about what is ‘actually’ occurring below our waking consciousness, wherein if we let our ‘guard’ down we will start ‘frothing at the mouth’? The writings can lead us beyond the ego-boundaries of cosy self-enclosure, but we must cease to self-reference everything that we are and do in the understanding that there is no self-nature. All these mad fictional selves hanging on the edge of oblivion is a scary picture is it not? So give it up! Turn to the Truth of us as the writings are always appealing us to do, by falling out of all our self-definitions, which is a knowing, i.e. we think we ‘know stuff’, the ‘gremlin mind’, and fall into the mystery of us. If we don’t feel that the ‘author’ is mad, then we need to ‘trust’ more, because the ‘author’ is not a self and if he is mad it’s as [[divine madness]], where in there is not a jot of a centre or a boundary.
