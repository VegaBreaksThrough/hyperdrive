As the usual conventional being we are all enthralled obsequiously with the defining touch of the psyche of this place, which is the unconscious grip of the ‘hunger’.
The ‘hunger’ is a disposition of mind that is wedded to meditation upon death which locks the bodily being, in the extreme cases, into a dark morbidity which gathers us up quickly, certainly and perhaps irretrievably and reduces us to a hapless wreckage of white lumber. The mind of the ‘hunger’, like those galaxies that apparently have black holes at their heart, which consume stars, and indeed other galaxies, sucks the life from its own bodily being and all the body that is near to its influence.
An individual trapped in the circumstance of the ‘hunger’ in its darkest ‘misfortune’ is in a state of collapse away from the life-force, the élan vital 1, like a black hole that consumes all aspects of attention granted to them without anything forthcoming, ever, from their terminal blackness. Those lost in the ‘hunger’ in these deadly cases are the true vampires of our fear. Individuals that have no life of their own responsibility but instead feed upon those who are demonstrating some limited relationship to the life-force at least.
Yet, while most people do not display extremes of the ‘hunger’, the usual disposition is tainted with much that is fitted to this collapse. This ‘hunger’ can best be seen in all the spurious desiring of the person and this is all those things or circumstances that ‘need’ to be added to life that are supposed through some magical arithmetic to amount to happiness. Such a quest of addition is endless and ultimately fruitless since we can never appease our ‘hunger’ with the things that at best only look good to eat but leave us empty and degraded. The ‘hunger’ can only end when we awaken to the understanding that our ‘hunger’ springs out of a dark core of fear at the heart of our being that carries us away from connecting to real nourishment that is to be found only when we fall out of our worship of fear.
Real nourishment lies beyond the mind of fear, which clutches at all things ‘hopefully’, in the realm of coming to the senses of trusting enough to allow bodily life to unfurl from its state of collapse and flower in the boundless fullness of real life.

1.	a term coined by Henri Bergson
