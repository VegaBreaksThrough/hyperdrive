The famous feud between the Montagues and the Capulets in Shakespeare’s play, ‘Romeo and Juliet’, is probably far more useful to unearthing the true nature of families than some fatuous situational television comedy that would have a nation chirruping trite unconsidered values in their aquarium ‘glug glugs’ all day long. Besides all this peristaltic gushing, the talk shows, with their supremos of idiocy and hype, further indict the family into greater and greater falsity as they are worshipped as purveyors of truth, whereas in [[Actuality]] they are merely pornographic seducers

The notion of family here considered is that primary mover of most of the ‘Western World’ and perhaps any gathering of people where they have some freedom beyond a pittance level of existence. The family structure is the means whereby governments maintain control of the nations of the bequest. This control is largely structured via the ownership of property and to this end the serious legalities of ownership are pursued with a great fervour. This pursuant interest by governments causes them to shape the dictates of the family that supposedly favours their continued existence and even their expansion into greater wealth and therefore so-called ‘greater happiness’. A part of this inculcated message suggests that the family has some intrinsic value that transcends its mere survival, that lifts the epithets of the family to the highest ideal in the land, so that nothing can be greater than to be a staunch supporter of the family and therefore nothing can be worse than to be at odds  in a black fleece. In this way the family becomes the harbinger of all the bonafide pursuits and the largely unspoken taboos. 
As with the warring Montagues and Capulets, most families subscribe to some fashionable involvement with religion, so that the good and righteous epithets of said religion flow by mere association into the so-called goodness and righteousness of each family member. To be good and righteous one only has to be seen in such apparent company. Those families calling themselves ‘Christians’ are definitely in trouble here since the teachings of Jesus of Nazareth do not support the family as the highest value and in fact totally despise the usual family (see Matthew 10:37, Mark 3:31-35, Luke 12:51-53, Luke 14:26). It is also little known that Jesus’ family, including his mother, despised him and thought him mad and made moves to have him ‘put away’. So, at least, with Christian families what they stand for rests on the shaky ground of poor discernment.

So perhaps we can now see why we have laboured so over this matter of family and this is because the nature of family, set in survival, precludes any matter that is even merely upsetting and makes an anathema of that which takes little heed of family relations, as in the vision of Real Life _, that lightly brushes their gravities aside. The family and indeed the pursuits of ordinary life are at war with the pursuit of Real Life which brokers no truck with the trifling matters of mere survival and distractions away from the way anything actually is.

This matter of real life is an awakening in consciousness that is a shift from the mere principle of self-survival (since it is clear that no one ultimately survives; such a principle is simply ad hoc) to the principle of self-sacrifice. Since it is also clear that everything passes, the principle of self-sacrifice is true to the ‘matter’ of all that comes to be and all that passes away. We can claim nothing as ours within the sticky clay of creation. The real life that gives rise to the principle of self-sacrifice, lived moment to moment, at last breaks our identification with all that is, with all that can die, and we are lifted beyond the one who must die, and therefore all desire that held us within the vision of the earthly plane. 

The family zeal, in their fear, would keep our real possibility from us, suggesting that pursuance of such an absolute is false or even evil, and that the truth is in the relativistic bosom of family life. Such a view is immoral since it would hold all family members in an addictive, exploitable, terrorised state rather than release them to ‘Families Anonymous’. The moral point of view invites all to discover their real possibility in any moment, and all moments, and that is the transcendence of all that is in the blissful [[Reality]] of Only God, until all that is, at last, simply slips away, barely noticed in our unending swoon.
