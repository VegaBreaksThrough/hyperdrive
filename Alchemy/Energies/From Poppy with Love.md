Much of the world in ‘Western societies’ has been reduced to acres and acres, mile upon mile, of concrete parking lots. In these concrete cities, the natural magic of existence has been reduced to boring repetition or dangerous mania. This manic penchant for making over the world into an extended highway and parking lot system is a reflection of what is occurring within the bodies of the usual individual. The concrete of the highways finds its way into all the tissues of the body so that it is possible (and it has been seen) that a lump of concrete the size of a ten year old’s fist can be built in the very centre of the human heart. What finds its way into such a primary centre of our existence also occurs throughout the body, in the joints and arteries, until we might as well stand in the park like a statue and let the pigeons cover us finally with the lime deposits of their own droppings.
