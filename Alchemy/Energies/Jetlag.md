Flying in an airplane at speeds often greater than 600 miles per hour is a scary and very reckless thing to do. The body does not like it at all; it shows the signs of nervous distress. When a plane is taking off or landing, the agitation of the often several hundred passengers dramatically increases. “Everybody knows the dice are loaded...” in favour of disaster at the times of taking off and landing. The usual disposition is mostly ‘cool’ i.e. the terrified body is crushed into submission by the emotionally disassociated mind. However, if we could really look into this, we would see something akin to the flash shots that are used in movies to show what someone is feeling or thinking. In this case, the flashes are shots of bloody strewn plane wreckage coming into the mind like strobe lighting. Hence, the ‘cool’, even jolly, traveller has this underlying subliminal intrusion, which begins to cook in the confined space of the interior of a modern day jet. When this condition is extended over 20 to 30 hours, the communication in the plane can be reduced to climbing up the walls in claustrophobic ‘battiness’. Added to this, we are being whisked away at alarming speed from our biological ground state. This temporal and spatial shift is disturbing to our bodily functions as any international traveller can attest. Many of us under the skin of our ‘coolness’ have become after several hours of flight, disoriented ‘travel ragers’. The usual person does not realise that travelling from one country to another is not a simple matter, since these countries as possible destinations are where the populations of those places are living out the special theatre of their karmic destiny. To find ourselves amid a place of destiny, perhaps quite unlike our destiny in ‘Woop Woop’, is to invite, through our unconscious desiring, negative circumstances to which we would otherwise not have been exposed. 
Thus travelling to other places of destiny is ‘dimensional travel’ and most often we cannot truly enter into the dimension to which we have travelled. We remain always uneasy, perhaps feeling threatened and rather like people watching a movie encapsulated in a bubble or looking out the window of the ‘Spaceship Hilton’.
