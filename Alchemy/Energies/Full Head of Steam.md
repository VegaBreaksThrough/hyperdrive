“...[[I Think Icon, I Think Icon, I Think Icon|I think I can, I think I can, I think I can]]...” says the little red engine puffing its way up a steep incline to a point of virtually bursting its boiler. It is this kind of adolescent exertion founded in self-will efforts that can bring us to the point of exploding under the pressure. The self-willed adolescent lives outside communion with the Divine and in this unenlightened state, the more he or she puffs and heaves, the more stress is placed on the structure, and particularly the circulatory system and the fine blood vessels in the head, until they are very much like the little red-faced engine. 
This consideration invites these adolescent characters to yield up their terrible struggle into the effortless bliss of divine communion. In this divine communion we are no longer using the present moment as a mere springboard into some future moment, but are living morally in the form of each moment as present enjoyment utterly sufficient unto itself.
