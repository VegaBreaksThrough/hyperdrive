It is usual for us to blame others and circumstances for how we feel or what is so-called happening to us. Is this a game of atonement ascertained by some ‘it’ intelligence set in some primitive response? Are we seeking to cleanse ourselves of an underlying feeling of guilt as a kind of hysterical relief? If we see our actions over and against a supposed Judgement Day, do we see how such an event would go for us? Would we pass through the moment to the ‘other side’? Most of us know where we stand. Given the way we live without intimacy with God, what else could we feel but guilt? If we fail to resolve this guilt in Truth, we are doomed to wander this earth as an angry uptight creature or even spirit, forever abiding in the stance of ‘It’s all your fault!’ - whatever we perceive to be worthy of blame and vengeance.
Although we tend to blame other people or circumstances for all our ‘problems’, all our Crisis! What Crisis!, can this ever really be the case? Even if we feel something positive such as, “You make me feel very happy!”, is this ever true? It is not true that anyone or anything can make us happy, if only because people die and things or circumstances disappear or alter significantly in our apprehension. This suggests we can never actually be made unhappy by all this loss and all we are being shown is that what can be lost can never be the source of happiness, since happiness is the Truth of us, our very being, which can obviously never be lost. The negative aspect of this - “You make me so angry!” - also cannot be true, since if any given person were truly the source of our anger, they would be the source of everyone’s ‘anger’ and we know that this is not true. Further we are most often alone in our reactions, even if others are more or less privy to the same condition. What is true is that our anger springs out of our interaction, which is uniquely self-dependent and because this is the case, our reaction of anger is simply arising and by creating a drama out of our reaction, we are left ‘alone’ with the ‘guilt of it’. 
So, is the scapegoat ever effective? Are we atoned and left feeling innocent of any wrong doing through blaming others? The response is clearly ‘no’, since everything we amount to can never be healed by reaction. Therefore, in the ordinary situation, no ‘one’ or no thing can take the burden of our responsibility for our reactions to living circumstances. These are purely and always our responsibility and this responsibility is to cease to blame anything but ourselves for anything that is apparently happening to us. No circumstance of ordinary life is ever actually good, so the so-called ‘good times’ are not a matter of self-congratulations. The only way beyond our reactions as an ever present false response, is to awaken at last to remorse for all the heartless activity we have generated in our stand outside the heart of Only God. In such remorse we feel the pain of our reactions and in feeling this pain, we f ind our true “sorry” and over time we simply see we can no longer engage the activity of this particular reaction.
The conclusion of the practice of the process of remorse is that we come to truly rest in responsibility for all reactions and this is the life lived in grace, wherein we see that all our reactions were fundamentally against God and that Only God can atone us for the burden of our lovelessness. No ordinary scapegoat sent into the desert can ever atone us. The only true scapegoat is the gift of the Lamb of God, who bears all for the sake of all, whenever such a one may appear.
