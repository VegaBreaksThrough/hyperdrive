Consider the Robert Browning poem, Rabbi Ben Ezra (Youth and Age) which says at its onset, 

“Grow old along with me The best is yet to be...”

And this, no matter how lauded by poetry lovers would seem to be the romantic ramblings of a deranged mind for those beset with any number of degenerative functional disorders and pushed aside by the world of the young in their headlong rush toward their own ruin into a desolate world of television reruns of ‘[[Surfing By The I Love Lucy Show|I Love Lucy]]’. Most of us faced with mounting numbers of painful wrinkles of time can only exclaim from the chair of our wreckage: “Old age has nothing to recommend it!” Further there is an irony to the poem Rabbi Ben Ezra, which is the author’s name (Browning) since one of the progenitors of the painful dysfunctions of growing old is glucose browning which is the unfortunate propensity of the body to literally cook at blood temperature. With a name like Browning there is little doubt that Robert became thoroughly ‘browned off’ with old age toward the end.

The search for a ‘cure’ to the aging process that those of us who live long enough must surely endure has been a major preoccupation of humanity since we had time to consider such things. Yet most of us simply drift unerringly and unthinkingly into the destiny that awaits us. The song writer Irving Berlin when he was some ninety-eight years old said, “If I had known I was going to live so long I would have taken better care of myself.” What could have been done during such a passage of time anyway? Could we eat better? Could we exercise more regularly? Stay out of the sun? Whilst these aspects do effect the quality of life, we enjoy up to a certain age it seems there is nothing we can do to prevent aging. In fact it is difficult to be clear as to what ageing is and how fast it is taking place for any given individual or why it is happening, since human cells can apparently live indefinitely. There is a circumspect (but reductionist) view of molecular biologists, which intimates that we are suffering a trade off by nature between reproduction and survival or that we are sailing into a Sargasso sea of ruinous mutant genes that create a mounting disarray across every level of our structural existence. There are many structural considerations no doubt from oxidation to glucose browning and so on. Yet while the erstwhile molecular biologist sees the onset of diseases and aging as somehow imperfect, from the point of view of karma only perfection is seen, since we are only encountering what we ‘deserve’ in this bardo world of promise we call, “Home Sweet Home.” Thus our particular Pop-Up story is the destiny of our born being as long as that destiny remains necessary. The destiny remains necessary as long as we are focused on trying to ‘save’ this little self from becoming “ashes to ashes, dust to dust.” We are lifted out of the grosser nasties of our destiny by awakening as a conscious sacrifice into the Mystery of us. In any case, the world is void of self-nature and we need to realise this at last and then the blessing of existence may descend upon that dependent event that is ‘us’.

The hermetic touch can aid in the dissolution of our karmas, our sin can be forgiven, but we must truly change what it is that we are up to and stop moving out of fear, and trust that our footsteps will alight somewhere even if we do not know where. What can also be seen is that ageing is only loosely linked to longevity. So, what we require of the structure is that it remains healthy until death do us part. Only those truly engaged in transcending the dream of existence may require a long and healthy life. The image of a two hundred year old surfie still trundling to the sea in search of the elusive sinusoidal orgasm is a problem that not even the usual mediocre personality needs. We are indeed fortunate that most of the dreadful personalities that are here for a time take their leave of us as quickly as they do. What a howdy-do if they did not. Thank God everyone who has ever lived has died.
