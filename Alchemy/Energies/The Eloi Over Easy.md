H.G. Wells’ Time Machine tells of a position where the remnants of the upper class, the Eloi, are portrayed as pretty little people who feed on fruit and merely play all day in the sun. The Eloi are totally oblivious to anything beyond the appearance of things. Under the world of the Eloi are the gruesome hairy remnants of the working class, the Morlocks, who provide the food for the Eloi, but in turn lure the hapless creatures into the entrances of their underworld, where they are butchered and eaten. This story is an allegory for the usual position of unconsciousness in this world. As unconscious beings, merely accepting the appearance of things, we are an unconscious sacrifice; quite simply we are eaten by all manner of evil feeders who ‘nurture’ us for their feeding purposes. We are permitted to involve ourselves with myriad possible frivolous distractions while we are being fattened for some unholy feasting. We must awaken from this unconscious dream and whilst everything is a sacrifice in this world, we must choose the position of a conscious sacrifice as a loving serving being, no longer willing to survive as dazed cattle awaiting the slaughter. At last, the ‘lunch fights back’, by abiding as love, forever, as the only discipline of existence.
