“I keep telling myself this; I keep telling the body that; I think I’m doing something effective by talking to myself and the body. I guess I’m expecting a profound response, but who’s doing the talking and who’s doing the listening and would I expect profound answers by talking to carrots?”
This is a common activity among many people, if not all. The talking to the self is typically a rationalisation process, such as, “Well, it wasn’t too bad eating that cream bun. After all; yesterday I ate two, and today I’ve only had one.” “So what if I flunked the exam. How important was it anyway?” And so it goes. Typically, we talk to the body to get it to pay attention, such as, “Relax, relax,” and, “There’s no reason to be so uptight.” However, the body is not alive and further it is not responsible for the uptightness and it has no way to respond to this type of prompting. The body is a vegetative function. We bring change to it, not by talking to it from the point of view of the mind, but by transcending the mind. The mind is in time; the body is in space. The body is the past and it reflects the activity of the mind - in transcending the mind in ecstatic communion with the Divine, the body at last follows into that communion. Likewise, talking to the self does not bring forth real discipline. Real discipline comes from the disposition of [[Real Observation]], which is already free, not simply trying to get free. [[Real Observation]] is therefore in divine communion and does not support the position of egoic self-indulgence and the mind’s rationalisation process relative to that self- indulgence. Therefore [[Real Observation]] is the intelligence, is the discipline, is the action.
