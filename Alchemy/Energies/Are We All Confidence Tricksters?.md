Trust and betrayal do not belong to the natural order of the world, but belong instead, almost exclusively, to the so-called human cultures. We can immediately hear pet owners screaming for attention, “I can trust my cat!”, “I can trust my dog!” No doubt there are many things we feel we can trust about animals. However, with an animal, we do not have a trust relationship and so it may be true we can ‘trust’ our dog, but our dog cannot ‘trust’ us. The dog may not be capable of betrayal, but we certainly are. So, trust is about an acknowledged relationship, yet in such a so-called relationship the de facto situation is found to be mostly betrayal of an array of expectations we had of our trustees: “I thought I could ‘trust’ that at least.”, “Who would have thought you could stoop so low!” So, mostly what we are calling trust is merely something we are thinking about someone or the faceless ‘someones’ behind the institutions we deal with. But we are simply naive and perhaps the same could be said about our animals. So, because for us, trust is just a notion based upon any number of cultural expectations which have no actual basis in fact, none of our so-called trust relationships can ever be tested or known a priori, since it is always contingent. Trust, however, is not merely a contingency. So, often women discover the men in their lives are ‘boundless rogues’ or ‘shameless dogs’, but actually they never were worthy of trust and were simply doing what ‘shameless dogs’ do, which is to say, that they do not enjoy the ‘creative freedom’ to do otherwise and men discover many oddities about the women they co-habit with that make their ‘fingers twitch’. 
In the ordinary worldly situation, trust is a much maligned word and at best all we can expect is that the reputations of the people we are dealing with matter more to them than the price of betrayal. Merely caring about our reputations is not about trust, since a reputation depends only upon what the general public know or don’t know. Trust, however, needs to depend upon some inherent quality.
What can we understand about trust beyond our cultural expectations? We see that trust simply cannot exist at the ordinary worldly level, because trust is more than just a pose, like a confidence trickster. And yet culturally we are all confidence tricksters, since we only play out a political form of trust in all our relationships in lieu of being able to trust ourselves or anyone or anything else. We discover quite universally that there is nothing trustworthy about this world, which includes what we amount to in our conventional stance. We cannot trust the ‘worldly’, because the worldly does not know itself for what it is; all of us in our unconscious condition are merely acting out a ‘parody of trust’, in which there is no possibility of integrity.
Trust in [[Reality]] must be an unshakable reliability that our obligations to one another at a functional level shall be met, ‘come hell or high water’. Clearly, such a demand upon us transcends what the ‘worldly’ is capable of. Thus, we see that we are ‘in’ the world, but if we are to know trust we cannot be of it. At a ‘worldly’ level we cannot trust even our body, since it can drop dead on us at any time. So real trust is a transcendent attribute and is simply never true of ordinary mortals. Our trustworthiness only exists when we are confessed in and as happiness, unshakably forever and are therefore alive as the presence of the eternal Divine. Therefore, only God is trustworthy and the acknowledged or truly known agents of that one.
