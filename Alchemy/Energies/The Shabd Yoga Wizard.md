***Etymology***

_Surat_ is "attention" or "face", that is, an outward expression of the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul "Soul");[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_Shabd_Yoga#cite_note-1) _[Shabd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabda "Shabda")_ or _Shabda_ has multiple meanings including ‘sacred song’, ‘word’, ‘voice’, ‘hymn’, ‘verse’, or ‘sound current, ‘audible life stream’, and the ‘essence of the Absolute Supreme Being’. The Absolute Supreme Being is a dynamic force of creative energy sent out into the abyss of space at the dawn of the universe's manifestation, as sound vibrations. These [vibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibration "Vibration") continue and are sent forth through the ages, framing all things that constitute and inhabit the universe.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_Shabd_Yoga#cite_note-2) _[Yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga "Yoga")_ is literally ‘union’, or ‘to yoke’. Etymologically, _Surat Shabd Yoga_ means the ‘Union of the Soul with the Essence of the Absolute Supreme Being’.

First is simran, or the repetition of Lord's holy names. It brings back our scattered attention to the tisra til - the third eye (behind our eyes), which is the headquarters of our mind and soul, in the waking state, whence it has scattered.

Second is dhyan, or contemplation on the immortal form of the Master. This helps in keeping the attention fixed at that centre.

Third is bhajan, or listening to the Anahad Shabd or celestial music that is constantly reverberating within us. With the help of this divine melody, the soul ascends to higher regions and ultimately reaches the feet of the Lord.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_Shabd_Yoga#cite_note-3)

_Surat Shabda Yoga_ is also known as _Sehaj Yoga_ – the path leading to _Sehaj_ or [equipoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrioception "Equilibrioception"), The Path of Light and Sound, The Path of the _[Sants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sants "Sants") or_ 'Saints', The Journey of Soul, and The Yoga of the Sound Current.

_Surat Shabda Yoga_ is for the discovery of _True Self_ ([Self-Realization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-realization "Self-realization")), _True Essence_ ([Spirit-Realization](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Spirit-Realization&action=edit&redlink=1 "Spirit-Realization (page does not exist)")), and _True Divinity_ ([God-Realization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God-realization "God-realization")) while living in the [human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") physical body. This involves reuniting in stages with what is called the "Essence of the Absolute Supreme Being", also known as the "Shabd or Word". Attaining this extent of self-realization is believed to result in _[jivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiva "Jiva")_ _[moksha/mukti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moksha "Moksha")_, which is liberation/release from _[samsara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsara "Samsara")_ and positivity in the cycle of [karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karma "Karma") and [reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation "Reincarnation"). Initiation by a contemporary living _[Satguru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satguru "Satguru")_ (Sat - true, [Guru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru "Guru") - teacher) is considered a prerequisite for successful _[sadhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadhana "Sadhana")_ (spiritual exercises). The _sadhanas_ include _[simran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simran_(Sanskrit_word) "Simran (Sanskrit word)")_ (repetition, particularly silent repetition of a _[mantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantra "Mantra")_ given at initiation), _dhyan_ (concentration, viewing, or contemplation, particularly on the Inner Master), and _[bhajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhajan "Bhajan")_ (listening to the inner sounds of the _Shabd_). The mantra is Guru Manter. These are some verbal words that one gets from living masters and chants in mind, not with the mouth. As the language varies from culture to culture, Guru Manter or Mantra may also vary from culture to culture in spirituality. On the other hand, Naam or Word of God or Kalma is unspeakable, alive and no one can write it on paper. In the new testament "Hebrews 4:12" states, "_For the Word of God is living and active. Sharper than any double-edged sword, it pierces even to dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow. It judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart._" Words which we write on paper are not alive and active. According to Scripture, the Word of God has existed since the beginning of the world, prior to any man-made language.

Surat Shabd Yoga arose in India in the last several hundred years, specifically in the Sikh tradition ([Nanakpanthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanakpanthi "Nanakpanthi")) founded by Guru Nanak. The practice of meditation (Shabad), which is the central core practice of Surat Shabd Yoga, is derived from the ancient Hindu practice of _[nāda yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81da_yoga "Nāda yoga")_. _Nada yoga_ is expounded in various Hindu scriptures such as the [Nadabindu Upanishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadabindu_Upanishad "Nadabindu Upanishad"), an ancient text affiliated with the several thousands-year-old [Rig Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rig_Veda "Rig Veda"). The practice of _nāda yoga_ within Hinduism has been widely affiliated within many yoga traditions including _[bhakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakti "Bhakti")_ or devotional yoga, _[kundalini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundalini_yoga "Kundalini yoga")_ and _[tantric yogas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantric_yoga "Tantric yoga")_, _[laya yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laya_yoga "Laya yoga")_, and _[raja yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_yoga "Raja yoga")_. Modern Hindu teachers still emphasizing _nada yoga_ include Swami Sivananda, Swami Rama, Rammurti Mishra (Shri Brahmananda Sarasvati), Paramahansa Yogananda (Kriya Yoga Lineage), and many others. The practice of _nāda yoga_ is an integral part of various other traditions as well, such as being a form of the advanced _[thogal](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Thogal&action=edit&redlink=1 "Thogal (page does not exist)")_ practice in the Tibetan [Dzogchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzogchen "Dzogchen") lineage, and is mentioned by [H. P. Blavatsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._P._Blavatsky "H. P. Blavatsky"), founder of the [Theosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophical_Society "Theosophical Society") in her book "The Voice of the Silence". The form of Surat Shabd Yoga, practiced by followers of Sant Mat and the Sikh tradition (_nanakpanthi_), is most commonly related to _nāda yoga_. Furthermore, _nāda yoga_ resembles and combines elements from the Hindu practices of _raja yoga_, _laya yoga_, and _bhakti yoga_.
