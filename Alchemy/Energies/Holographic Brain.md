The “Holographic Brain” refers to the theory proposed by **Karl Pribram**, a neuroscientist, who suggested that the brain processes information in a way similar to a hologram. This theory challenges traditional views of localized brain functions and instead posits that:

1. **Memory and perception are distributed**: Information is stored and processed holistically across the brain, meaning any part of the brain can potentially reconstruct the whole.

2. The brain uses **interference patterns**, akin to those in holography, to encode and retrieve information.

3. It aligns with the idea that mental processes (like vision, memory, and problem-solving) involve complex patterns rather than isolated neural circuits.

  

Pribram’s ideas emerged in the 1960s and were influenced by developments in quantum physics and **David Bohm’s [[Holomovement]] theory**
