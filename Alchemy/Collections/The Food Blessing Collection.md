[[The Consideration of Certified Organic, Fair Trade, Cruelty Free Foods and Food Miles.]]

[[Certified Organic]]

Organic produce utilises no chemical pesticides, insecticides, herbicides, fungicides, synthetic fertilisers or genetically modified organisms, some or all of which are used in non-organic produce. There are several bodies of certifiers for organics depending on where we reside, yet the main vision for all organic farmers is to work in harmony with nature, aligning to ecologically sustainable practices taking into account our land, water and air. This goes hand in hand with creating a healthy environment for the animals and workers alike.

As a result of continuing with the use of chemicals and harsh agricultural practices, deserts and water ‘dead zones’ are appearing; where nothing lives. In supporting the cessation of the use of harmful toxic substances and incorporating more sustainable farming methods, the earth can regenerate over time.

[[Fair Trade]]

This movement advocates the payment of a fair price for product and labour (non exploitation). Fair Trade’s strategic intent is to deliberately work with marginalised producers and workers in order to help them move from a position of vulnerability to one of security and economic self-sufficiency. Much of our food comes from other countries. By buying Fair Trade food and drink, we can go a long way to making sure the farmers who produce it are not exploited.

[[Cruelty Free]]

Cruelty Free organisations focus on the prevention of cruelty to animals. Most of us eat killed food in the form of fish, chicken or red meat. We need to know how these animals are raised and how they are slaughtered. All of this must amount to being cruelty free. Unconscious practices and factory farming methods often incorporate levels of torture to the animals. Cruelty free is just that.

[[Food Miles]]

This refers to the consideration of how many miles food has travelled to reach our plate and how much energy has been expended to get it to us. Where possible, we can source locally grown foods to minimise this. Many food shops provide us with the information of where the produce came from on the display and/or packaging to help us choose.
