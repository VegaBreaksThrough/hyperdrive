# Universidad Del Alqvimia

It is a higher human faculty that enables understanding, comprehension and reverence for God. The Mind's Eye is the Light of understanding that creates reality.

  

In Hermetica, the following is said about it: “(...) the one who created everything; who ﬁxed the earth in place; who hung heaven above; who ordered the sweet water away from the ocean and toward land, the habitable and the uninhabitable, as the means of mankind's nourishment and creation; who ordered ﬁre to shine on gods and humans for their every use. Together let us praise him, raised high above the heavens, creator of all nature. He is the Mind's Eye(...)”

# Wikipedia

The notion of a "mind's eye" goes back at least to [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero "Cicero")'s (1st Century BC) reference to _**mentis oculi**_ during his discussion of the orator's appropriate use of [simile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simile "Simile").

In this discussion, Cicero observed that allusions to "the [Syrtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Gab%C3%A8s "Gulf of Gabès") of his patrimony" and "the [Charybdis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charybdis "Charybdis") of his possessions" involved similes that were "too far-fetched"; and he advised the orator to, instead, just speak of "the rock" and "the gulf" (respectively)—on the grounds that "the eyes of the mind are more easily directed to those objects which we have seen, than to those which we have only heard".

The concept of "the mind's eye" first appeared in English in [Chaucer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer "Geoffrey Chaucer") (c. 1387) [Man of Law's Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_of_Law%27s_Prologue_and_Tale "The Man of Law's Prologue and Tale") in his _[Canterbury Tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Canterbury_Tales "The Canterbury Tales")_, where he tells us that one of the three men dwelling in a castle was blind, and could only see with "the eyes of his mind"; namely, those eyes "with which all men see after they have become blind".[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_image#cite_note-24)
