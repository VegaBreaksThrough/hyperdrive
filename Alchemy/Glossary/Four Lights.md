In the context of advanced Tibetan Buddhist practices like the **[[Six Yogas]] of Naropa** and **[[A0195|Mahamudra meditation]]**, the **Four Lights** refer to profound experiences of awareness and luminosity that arise during deep meditative absorption and also during the process of dying. These states are connected to the realization of the **clear light nature of mind**, which is luminous, empty, and unchanging.

  

**The Four Lights:**

1. **Light of Appearance (Dang)**:

• Represents the initial, gross level of clarity in meditation or during the dissolution process at death.

• It is the experience of subtle forms, thoughts, or visions arising in the mind.

• In meditation: This light corresponds to the vividness of the practitioner’s visualization or awareness.

• At death: It arises when the elements of the physical body begin to dissolve, and the outer world fades.

2. **Light of Increase (Gyäwa)**:

• The light becomes brighter and more refined, representing the dissolving of gross dualistic perception.

• In meditation: Awareness intensifies as attachment to mental constructs diminishes.

• At death: This occurs as the inner winds (_prana_) and energies begin to collect into the [[Subtle Body|central channel]], deepening the clarity of mind.

3. **Light of Attainment (Nyam Nyid)**:

• A subtler, more profound luminosity arises, indicating proximity to the realization of ultimate reality.

• In meditation: The practitioner begins to recognize the emptiness underlying all appearances.

• At death: This corresponds to the further dissolution of mental faculties, with only subtle consciousness remaining.

4. **Clear Light (Ösel)**:

• The ultimate, primordial light of **pure awareness** (_rigpa_), where all dualistic perception is dissolved.

• In meditation: The practitioner directly experiences the non-dual, luminous, and empty nature of mind.

• At death: This is the final stage, corresponding to the full merging with the **clear light of the Dharmakaya**, which enlightened beings recognize as their true nature.

  

**Connection to Mahamudra:**

  

In Mahamudra practice, these Four Lights represent stages of increasingly refined awareness leading to the realization of the **nature of mind**. The ultimate goal is to stabilize the recognition of the Clear Light as the ground of all experience, both in life and during the transitional states of death.
